Reframes — what if booking wasn't a menu?
Every booking tool copies the salon till: a list of services, a price, a calendar. But a nail tech's customers don't think in services — they think in looks, in her, and in when I'm next due. And the techs don't think in catalogues; they think in Instagram posts and DMs. Eight experiments below, each borrowing a pattern from another industry and asking what it does to the loop. Tags: MVP works on data you already store; +1 FIELD needs one new column or link; DREAM needs a real feature.
THE LOOP THESE ADD UP TO
She finishes a set→ photographs it from the booking (07)→ photo is tagged with what made it→ next customer books the look (01)→ arrives with a brief she already understands (03)→ three weeks later, rebooked in one tap (02)
Acuity and Fresha have none of this because they were built for salons with menus. The portfolio becomes the catalogue, the booking becomes the portfolio's source, and the calendar stops being the front door.

What I'd take into the MVP, in order
05 Booking is home and 02 the ritual — zero schema, and they make a returning customer's app feel like hers. 07 close with a photo next: one link (image → booking → treatment) that pays for itself immediately in portfolio growth, then unlocks 01 book the look and 06 the feed for free. 09 the sentence is a weekend of parsing. 04 search by when is the one I'd test rather than assume — it contradicts your no-dates call, and your sister's customers can settle it. 03 the brief and 08 the import are the two real features; if only one, the import, because it's the reason providers arrive, and the brief is what they do in DMs today anyway.
