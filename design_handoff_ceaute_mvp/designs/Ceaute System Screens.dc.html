System screens — email, legal, failures
Everything the app does outside the two happy paths. Emails are rebuilt as letters rather than receipts. Then the three checkout bounce-backs, the auth error, not-found and the crash page, all grounded in the routes that exist. Legal and the root-route decision are at the bottom.
EMAIL · REBUILT
What was wrong with the first version
It was a receipt: a coloured bar, a pill badge, a caps micro-label over every block, and thirteen grey-label-plus-bold-value rows. That layout treats every fact as equally important, so the two things she actually needs at 13:50 on Friday — the time and the address — are buried among nine things she does not. It also looks like software, not like her nail tech.
What these do instead
The Confirmed screen she liked is the model: a second-person sentence, then the time set large, then the address as prose, then money as one line. No badges, no tables, one uppercase label per email at most. Every fact from the content file is still present and in the same order — Booking, Payment, Where — just weighted by what gets acted on. All of it is still tables and inline styles underneath, so nothing here needs CSS an email client would drop.

What Ceaute is
Ceaute is a booking platform that connects independent beauty providers with customers. Ceaute is not a beauty business itself and does not perform, supervise, or guarantee any treatment.
Providers and customers
Each provider on Ceaute is an independent business responsible for the treatments they list, the prices they set, and the standard of the appointment itself. The contract for a treatment is between you and the provider, not Ceaute. Ceaute is responsible for the booking, payment, and cancellation systems described below.
Your account
You need an account to book a treatment or to publish a provider page. One account covers both — creating a provider page does not create a second account. We sign you in with a one-time code sent to your email address; there is no password to protect. You must give us a real, accessible email address and are responsible for anything done from your account.
Cancellation deadlines and charges
Each provider sets a cancellation window of 12, 24, or 48 hours, and may add their own written cancellation policy. Both are shown to you before you pay. If you cancel before the deadline, the amount you paid online is refunded in full. If you cancel after the deadline, the provider keeps up to the deposit or full payment amount shown at booking, and any remainder is refunded. Ceaute does not currently support rescheduling a booking; changing a booking means cancelling it, subject to these charges, and booking again.
If a provider cancels
If a provider cancels a confirmed booking, the full amount you paid online is refunded. Refunds are returned to your original payment method through Stripe; they can take a few business days to appear and, rarely, may be delayed while Ceaute retries a refund after a payment processor outage — the cancellation itself still takes effect immediately.

THE ROOT ROUTE · CEAUTE.COM/
Redirect, but not unconditionally
Right now page.tsx renders an empty main, so the root is a blank page — the worst of the options. Redirecting to Discover is right for a signed-out visitor: there is nothing a landing page would say that Discover does not say better with real providers on it, and an MVP with twelve providers cannot afford a marketing page nobody maintains.
WHAT / SHOULD DO, BY WHO ARRIVES
Signed out
→ /discover
The providers are the product. Category chips and the search box are a better first screen than a pitch.
Signed in, customer
→ /discover
Same place, with the avatar menu available. Once the booking-led home from Reframes 05 exists this becomes /account — not for launch.
Signed in, has a page
→ /dashboard
A provider typing the bare domain is going to work, not shopping. Discover is still one tap away in the nav.
Mid-booking, hold live
→ back to the held booking
Losing a slot because someone tapped the logo mid-flow is the one genuinely costly case.
Two implementation notes. Make it a server-side redirect so there is never a blank frame, and keep it temporary rather than permanent — a marketing page will eventually live at the root, and a permanent redirect stays cached in browsers for a long time. Second, the wordmark in the top bar should link to /discover for customers and /dashboard for providers rather than to the root, so the redirect is a fallback for typed URLs rather than something users hit constantly.
